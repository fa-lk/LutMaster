LutMaster Help

Choose a chapter. Each guide explains what the controls do and when to use them.

Photos & preview

  • Add photos: Drop image files onto the empty workspace, or click it. Use Add photos to add more later. The built-in test chart is a sample image for trying the controls.
  • Choose a photo: Click its thumbnail. Remove photo removes the selected photo from the workspace.
  • Zoom and move: Use the mouse wheel to zoom, then drag the image to look around. On a touchscreen, pinch to zoom and drag with one finger. Double-click the image to fit it in the preview again.
  • Before / after: Hold Space to see the original photo; release it to return to your edit. Press S for a movable split line between original and edited.
  • Warnings and histogram: C marks areas pushed to pure black or white. H shows a graph of how much dark, bright and coloured information the photo contains.
  • Preview quality: Std is faster while editing. Full uses the original image resolution where possible; it needs more memory and processing.
  • Fullscreen: The ⛶ button expands LutMaster to fill the screen.
Your photos are processed in the browser. They are not uploaded by LutMaster.

Basic adjustments

Start with Tone for brightness and contrast, then use Colour to adjust the overall colour. Small changes are usually easier to control.

  • Section checkbox: Temporarily turns that whole section on or off. Your slider values stay there. Reset clears only that section; Reset all returns the current edit to its defaults. It does not delete the looks you have saved.
  • Sliders and numbers: Drag a slider for a quick change, or type a number for precision. Double-click a value or slider to return it to its default.
  • Exposure: Makes the whole photo lighter or darker.
  • Contrast: Makes dark and bright parts further apart or closer together.
  • Highlights / Shadows: Adjust the bright parts / dark parts without changing the whole image as much.
  • Blacks: Changes the darkest tones. Gamma mainly changes the middle brightness. Fade blacks raises the darkest tones for a faded look.
  • Temperature: Moves the image between cooler blue and warmer yellow. Tint moves between green and magenta.
  • Saturation: Makes all colours stronger or weaker. Vibrance mainly boosts or reduces colours that are not already strong.
  • Protect skin: Reduces the effect of the Colour adjustments on likely skin tones. Normalisation gently evens out colour-channel balance.
  • A / B: Keep two edit versions for comparison. Switch with the A and B buttons (or arrow keys when no point is selected). copy → copies the current version into the other slot.

Automatic corrections

These buttons analyse the selected photo and suggest settings. They are starting points: check the result and adjust it yourself.

  • Auto Grade: Automatically adjusts the overall tone. Click it again to remove that automatic result.
  • Auto Grade – Subject: Draw a closed line around the subject, double-click or press Enter to finish, then press Apply. LutMaster increases contrast on the subject while trying to keep the rest of the scene's brightness. Cancel discards the selection.
  • Grade Colors: Analyses the photo and automatically adjusts the three Color Grading wheels and colour saturation by hue. Click it again to restore the settings from before the automatic grade.
  • Grading intensity: Controls how strong Grade Colors is. Protect Hue reduces an unwanted overall colour shift. Protect Skin reduces the automatic wheel effect on skin-like colours.
  • Exposure: Finds a starting overall brightness. Recover tries to bring back bright and dark detail.
  • Shadow detail / Highlight roll-off: Opens up dark detail / softens very bright tones.
  • Colour / White bal.: Estimates colour strength / removes an overall colour cast.
  • Skin tone / Luma saturation: Gently adjusts likely skin colours / balances colour strength from shadows to highlights.

Curves & colour selection

  • RGB / Red / Green / Blue curve: Choose RGB to change brightness, or a colour channel to change that colour. The left-to-right position is the original value; the up-and-down position is the result. Above the diagonal makes it brighter; below makes it darker.
  • Add or move a point: Click the curve to add a point, then drag it. Double-click a point to remove it. The two end points stay fixed.
  • Add point from image: Press G or click the button, then click the photo. The small circle shows the sampled area. The new point starts on the existing curve, so adding it alone does not change the picture.
  • Hue vs Hue: Changes the actual colour of a colour range (for example, shift yellow leaves towards green).
  • Hue vs Saturation: Makes a chosen colour stronger or weaker without changing the other colours as much.
  • Luma vs Saturation: Changes colour strength according to brightness, from dark areas on the left to bright areas on the right.
  • Secondary colour: Pick a target colour in the photo. Range decides which nearby colours are included; Softness makes the edge gentler. Then adjust its hue, saturation or luminance.
  • White balance picker: Press W or click the picker, then click something that should be neutral grey or white. Press W again to turn the picker off.

Color grading

  • Color wheel: Adds a colour tint to Shadows (dark areas), Mids (middle tones) or Highlights (bright areas). Drag a marker to change that range. The centre means no tint.
  • Select a range: Tap a marker to select it for touch controls; tap it again to leave edit mode. You can also drag any marker directly without selecting it first.
  • Arrow keys: When a wheel marker is selected, the arrow keys move it. Left/right changes its horizontal position; up/down changes its vertical position.
  • Hue / Sat buttons: Rotate the selected colour or increase/decrease its strength in small steps.
  • Blending: Controls how smoothly the Shadows, Mids and Highlights ranges overlap. Lower values separate them; higher values make the transition smoother.
  • Balance: Shifts the overall grading emphasis towards shadows (negative) or highlights (positive).
  • Split toning: Adds one chosen colour to dark areas and another to bright areas. Pick the two colours and raise their strength sliders to see the effect.
  • Transfer ramp: Shows the current edit applied to a simple black-to-white gradient, from dark on the left to bright on the right.

Looks & pipeline

  • Pipeline: Shows the transforms used to make the final image. The Base LUT is applied first; the layers below it are applied in order.
  • Layer controls: Untick a layer to switch it off without deleting it. Drag the ⋮ handle to change its order. Change its strength to blend it in. Press × to remove it.
  • Save: Saves your current adjustments as a reusable look inside LutMaster. It does not download a file. Saved looks stay in this browser.
  • Add .cube file: Imports a LUT file made by another app or downloaded from elsewhere. Select a saved look or imported LUT to add it to the Pipeline.
  • Estimate from one photo: Analyses a reference photo and creates approximate starting settings for tone, white balance, colour and split toning.
  • Import Lightroom preset: Reads supported colour settings from an .xmp or .lrtemplate preset. The result is an approximation because Lightroom edits camera RAW data, while a LUT changes an already-rendered image.

Export & projects

  • Save photo: Exports the selected photo with the current edit. JPG 93 is a good smaller file; JPG 100 keeps more detail but is larger; PNG is lossless and usually largest.
  • Save all photos: Exports every photo in the workspace using the same current settings.
  • Export LUT (.cube): Creates a reusable colour transform for other software or supported cameras. Look strength controls how much of the edit is included. Choose a name and size: 17 is smaller, 33 is a common quality/size balance, and 65 is larger and more detailed.
  • Base Photo Style: For compatible Lumix cameras, choose the Photo Style used when the footage was recorded. The LUT can then carry that information with it.
  • Export look pack: Downloads all saved looks as separate .cube files.
  • Cube analysis: Shows a visual summary of the current colour transform. Change the 17³ / 33³ / 65³ view size to inspect it at different detail levels.
  • Save project / Load project: Saves or restores your editing setup, including adjustments and the Pipeline. Use this to continue working later. It is not the same as exporting a finished photo or LUT.
  • Undo / Redo: Undo reverses the last edit; Redo brings it back. Reset all resets the current edit.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Space hold: show the original photo. Release to return to the edit.
  • S: toggle Split Compare. C: toggle Clipping. H: toggle Histogram.
  • ↑ / ↓: previous / next photo when no editing point is selected.
  • ← / →: switch to A / B settings when no editing point is selected.
  • G: turn Add point from image on or off. Then click the photo to add a curve point.
  • W: turn the White Balance picker on or off. Then click a neutral area in the photo.
  • When a curve point is selected: arrow keys move the point. Left/right changes its input position; up/down changes its output.
  • When a Color Grading point is selected: arrow keys move the marker on the colour wheel.
  • Ctrl / ⌘ + Z: Undo. Add Shift for Redo; Ctrl / ⌘ + Y also redoes.
  • Enter finishes the subject outline and applies its correction; Esc cancels it. In save/export dialogs, Enter confirms and Esc cancels.
Arrow keys edit the selected point first. Photo navigation and A/B switching work only when no curve or Color Grading point is selected. Keyboard shortcuts are ignored while typing in text fields.
